# Characters

## The Player (You)

Uncle Tony's heir — niece/nephew, no automotive experience, no knowledge of "the family business." First day on the job. Player-facing name/gender not yet defined; scripts should default to neutral ("kid," "you," or addressed by last name) unless we decide otherwise.

## Uncle Tony (deceased)

- Owner of Tony's Garage, player's late uncle.
- Secretly a philanthropist: for years, ran a covert operation funding free/anonymous car repairs for people in crisis (job loss, divorce, no income, etc.), in partnership with the local Chief of Police.
- Appears only via recordings/photos during the investigation, then in person (recorded video) at the finale through the mascot's monitor — his final message and the YES/NO ask.
- Voice/personality: warm, wise, a little mischievous about keeping his secret — "sorry I couldn't tell you about this earlier, it was my little secret."

## Marion Cole (Estate Lawyer — voice only)

- Never seen. Heard on the **shop phone answering machine** at game start (blinking message light → player presses Play).
- Handled Tony's estate; short, warm framing of the inheritance before Frank takes over on the intercom.
- Purely a framing device for the opening beat.

## Frank (General Manager)

- Speaks via a **wall-mounted shop intercom** (audio) — **ongoing** through the room, not only the opening. Visual presence is the fake upstairs window (shadow / eyes).
- Cuts in immediately after Marion Cole's message ends — he overheard it and is annoyed ("always with the speeches"). Gruff, deep-voiced, mafia-tough-guy look when glimpsed upstairs. Later lines: pressure, assists, story beats (see wall-intercom prop doc).
- Has run the day-to-day under Tony for 30 years.
- Resents that Tony left the garage to the player instead of him — but begrudgingly tells the player to get to work.
- Delivers the game's inciting instruction: "follow in Tony's footsteps... and don't forget to take care of that dead thing in the trunk of the car."
- Watches the player from an upstairs window throughout the investigation — a recurring ominous presence/red herring suggesting he's dangerous or surveilling the player for the "family."
- Payoff: he's not a threat — just a loyal, overlooked employee. If the player says **NO** at the end, Tony hands the Manager the job after all, which recontextualizes his resentment as grief/loyalty rather than menace.
- **Design note:** he should read as a credible threat for most of the room so the "mob" misdirect lands. His ending should retroactively make his gruffness sympathetic.

## "The Don" — Dawn Adams, Chief of Police

- The person referenced in recordings discussing "the Don" — a classic mob-boss-sounding title.
- Actually **Dawn Adams**, the local Chief of Police, and Tony's real-life partner in the secret charity operation.
- Appears in a handshake photo with Tony on the wall (initially read as evidence of corruption/collusion — "why is the chief shaking hands with a mob guy" — actually evidence Tony was doing something good enough to have police blessing/support).

## The People Tony Helped (not present, referenced via evidence)

- Photos with people's faces crossed out, captioned "been taken care of" — initially read as hits/threats; actually customers whose situations were resolved (cars fixed, help delivered).
- Filing cabinet full of letters from people "down and out" asking for help.
- Specific examples referenced in the calendar/overnight work logs:
  - A man who lost his job
  - A divorced woman working two jobs
  - An old man with no income

## Sparky (the Mascot / "the dead thing in the trunk")

- **A monkey holding a wrench**, Tony's Garage's mascot figure — nametag reads "Sparky." Ties the "grease monkey" mechanic slang directly to the overall *Monkey Business* brand.
- Found lifeless in the trunk of a car, referenced ominously by Frank at the start ("don't forget to take care of that dead thing in the trunk").
- Missing a specific battery, which the player picks up early in the game without knowing its purpose.
- Once the trunk is unlocked (reward for solving the mob mystery) and the battery is installed, Sparky is placed on the Mascot Mount, triggering the finale monitor/Tony's message.
- Whether he's a static figure (battery just powers an internal light/sound) or a simple animatronic (moves slightly) is a build decision for later — see [`../05-Software-Electronics/`](../05-Software-Electronics/README.md).
