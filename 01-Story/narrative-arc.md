# Narrative Arc: Tony's Garage

## Act 1 — Arrival (Setup)

- Player enters as a novice mechanic heir — new to the industry, inherited the shop, doesn't know the trade.
- Marion Cole (answering machine) → **Frank** on wall intercom: gruff, seems not to like you; sends you to work.
- Inciting line: *"Follow in Tony's footsteps… and don't forget to take care of that dead thing in the trunk of the car."*
- Plants: (a) trunk/Sparky goal, (b) ominous "dead thing / take care of," (c) act like Tony — before you know what Tony was really like.

## Core Story Law — Player Assumption, Not Written Accusation

**The room never states that Tony is in the mob.** No prop, letter, or recording should say mafia / mob / hit / organized crime as fact. Players **incorrectly assume** that from vibe + ambiguous language. The a-ha is realizing *they* wrote the crime story — each clue actually tells a different (good) story.

Full pacing: [`../03-Flow/suspicion-curve.md`](../03-Flow/suspicion-curve.md).

## Act 2 — Main + Storage (Deepen the Wrong Story)

Early tasks and finds should **push deeper into the dark assumption**, not toward the truth. Frank watches from the upstairs window. By the end of **Storage**, the player should feel: *"I inherited something shady — maybe worse."* Still: nothing has *said* "mob."

**Evidence (player's misread → what the text actually allows):**

| Evidence | What players tend to assume | What the text actually says / shows |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden cash | Dirty / laundered money | Cash exists; source unexplained |
| Recording: "the Don," "take care of" the old man | Mob boss + a hit | Ambiguous slang + concern about an old man |
| Photos crossed out, "been taken care of" | Hits / people eliminated | People marked done; no method stated |
| Handshake with unnamed Chief of Police | Corrupt cop on the payroll | Friendly photo; no caption explaining why |
| Letters from people "down and out" | Extortion targets / favors owed | People asking for help |
| Overnight "off books" calendar | Criminal cover | Night work, no invoice |

**Pacing:** Main plants unease; Storage is peak wrong certainty. **Do not start the reframe in Storage.**

## Access Order (confirmed)

**Zones** gated: **Storage first → Office second.** Last major **container**: **car trunk**. (Trunk is not a zone — see [`../02-Room-Design/zones-and-containers.md`](../02-Room-Design/zones-and-containers.md).) Each gate plus smaller containers (lockers, cabinets, drawers) involves real puzzles, not freely-readable evidence alone.

## Clue Triangulation Mechanic

This is the core storytelling technique for the "reveal," and it's a pattern worth reusing elsewhere in the room: scatter individually-harmless facts across different zones so that **no single clue is damning or revealing on its own** — only combining 2-3 of them across zones produces the insight. This makes the Storage Closet → Office access order meaningful (you can't triangulate until you've been in both) and rewards players for connecting information themselves rather than just being told.

**Flagship example — realizing "the Don" is Dawn Adams:**

| # | Clue | Zone | What it tells you *alone* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photo of Uncle Tony (so players know his face) | Main Garage Bay | Just "this is what Tony looked like." Nothing suspicious by itself. |
| 2 | Recording of Tony and "the Don" discussing "taking care of" the old man who can't keep up with his job | Main | Ambiguous slang + an old man — players often *assume* crime; text never says it |
| 3 | Photo of Tony shaking hands with the (unnamed) Chief of Police | Storage | Friendly photo only; players may assume corruption once they've already assumed Tony is shady |
| 4 | Photo of the Chief of Police, labeled **Dawn Adams** | Office | Gives the Chief a first name — "Dawn" — first crack in the assumption |

**The triangulation:** #3 + #4 = handshake person is Dawn Adams; + #2 = **"the Don" = Dawn**. One of several a-has — collapses the *assumed* mob-boss into "partner is the Chief," and helps re-read the old man as helped, not targeted.

**Design implication / concept:** since no single room hands the player the full answer, the realization must be *used* in-game — not only mental. Drafted as puzzle **03 Partner Line** (seat DAWN over "THE DON" on Tony's Office desk): [`../04-Puzzles/03-partner-line-dawn/puzzle-concept.md`](../04-Puzzles/03-partner-line-dawn/puzzle-concept.md).

## Midpoint Turn — Starts in the Office

**The turn begins when the player enters the Office** — not before. Office is where assumptions start to crack. Prefer a **cascade of a-has** (each old clue re-reads differently) over one dump that explains everything.

| Earlier clue (Main/Storage) | Office (or Office-enabled) re-read |
|---|---|
| "The Don" | **Dawn Adams**, Chief of Police — a name, not a title (triangulation above) |
| People "taken care of" | Customers helped; situations resolved |
| Overnight calendar | Free/anonymous repairs at night for people in crisis |
| Hidden cash | Anonymous donor fund ("the family") for parts/labor |
| Handshake with Chief | Partnership in the charity, not a payoff |
| Down-and-out letters | Real help requests Tony answered |

**Design intent:** every misreadable clue from Main/Storage must remain *literally true* under the good reading. The crime story was never on the page — only in the player's head.

## Act 3 — Opening the Trunk Container

- Having pieced together the truth, the player opens the **car trunk** (container in Main).
- Inside: the "dead thing" — **Sparky**, lifeless, missing a specific battery.
- The player has been carrying that exact battery since near the start of the game (a "held item" that only makes sense in hindsight — a very intentional long-lead payoff).

## Finale — The Mantle

1. Player installs the battery into the mascot.
2. Places the mascot on the mantle "where it belongs."
3. A small monitor beneath/on the mantle lights up: **Tony**, recorded, addressing the player directly.
4. Tony's message (paraphrased): acknowledges the player probably uncovered his secret; apologizes for not telling them sooner; reframes the inheritance — it's not the money or the garage, it's the *responsibility*. He asks: **"Are you ready to join the family?"**
5. Two lit buttons appear: **YES / NO**.

### Ending A — NO

- Tony says he understands; it's a heavy responsibility; he loves the player all the same.
- He passes the role to the **Manager** instead.
- Tone: warm, a little bittersweet, still a "win."

### Ending B — YES

- Tony smiles big.
- Photos of the people he's helped light up around the room.
- Music starts, room lighting brightens.
- The Garage's sign — **"Tony's Garage"** — is joined by a second illuminated sign underneath: **"...IS FOR FAMILY."**
- Tone: full emotional payoff, triumphant/warm.

## Design Notes for Later Phases

- The final fork (YES/NO) is a **values-based, not skill-based**, ending — see [`../03-Flow/puzzle-dependency-map.md`](../03-Flow/puzzle-dependency-map.md).
- Zone access is gated (Storage → Office); car trunk is the last major container gate — see [`../03-Flow/puzzle-dependency-map.md`](../03-Flow/puzzle-dependency-map.md).
- The Act 2 → Midpoint evidence-reframing table above, plus the triangulation table, are effectively a punch list of puzzles/discoveries we need: each row is a potential puzzle or clue delivery moment.
- Finale requires two distinct show-control sequences (YES vs. NO) — flag for [`../05-Software-Electronics/`](../05-Software-Electronics/README.md) (lighting, audio/music, dual signage, monitor video playback, button inputs).
- The "held item with no apparent purpose until the very end" (the battery) is a great model for other long-lead items/clues in this room.
