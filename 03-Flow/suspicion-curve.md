# Suspicion Curve & Assumption Design

How the story *feels* across zone access — and the hard rule that makes the a-ha land.

## Hard Rule: The Room Never Says "Mob"

**Nothing the player finds or reads should state that Tony is in the mob, the mafia, organized crime, a hitman, etc.**

Players should **incorrectly assume** that themselves — from tone, Frank, slang, and ambiguous phrasing. When the truth lands, the a-ha is: *"I wrote that story in my head. Every clue actually told a different story."*

| Allowed (ambiguous) | Forbidden (explicit) |
|---|---|
| "the Don" | "mob boss," "capo," "mafia" |
| "taken care of" | "hit," "whacked," "body" (as crime) |
| "off books," "no paper trail," "the family" | "laundering," "front for the family" as stated fact |
| Cash in a weird place | A note that says "dirty money from jobs" |
| Handshake with a cop, no caption explaining why | "payoff photo" labeled as such |

Prop copy, recordings, and Frank's lines must obey this. Frank can be gruff and ominous; he should not say "your uncle was connected."

## Emotional / Assumption Arc by Zone

```
Main (start)
  You: new to the trade, inherited a shop, Frank seems to dislike you,
       "dead thing in the trunk," odd slang — unease grows.
  Job of early puzzles: PUSH DEEPER into the dark assumption.
       More ambiguous "bad" texture. Never confirm mob in writing.

Storage (first gated zone)
  Peak wrong assumption. Handshake photo, letters, crossed-out faces,
  cash — still never says "mob," but the player's theory feels airtight.
  Exit Storage believing: "I inherited something shady."

Office (second gated zone)  ← TURN STARTS HERE
  First cracks. Dawn Adams nameplate, ledger language, calendar context,
  second half of a recording — players begin to realize THEY assumed wrong.
  Not one dump of "here's the truth" — a cascade of a-has as each old clue
  re-reads as charity / partnership / help.

Trunk container → Finale
  Open with clear eyes. Sparky. Tony asks you to join "the family"
  (now meaning something warm). YES/NO.
```

## Design Implications for Clues

1. **Main + Storage clues** should be *optimally misreadable* — a reasonable person jumps to crime; a careful re-read later still fits the good reading with zero contradiction.
2. **Office clues** are the first that *don't* fit the mob story (or only fit it with strain). That dissonance starts the turn.
3. **Each major misread clue needs a payoff a-ha** — not one big speech that explains everything. Prefer: player reinterprets clue A, then B, then C (each its own mini-reveal).
4. **Triangulation** (e.g. Don → Dawn) is one a-ha among several; it should unlock or accompany others, not replace them.
