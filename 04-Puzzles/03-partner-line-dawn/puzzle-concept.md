# Puzzle: Partner Line ("Call the Don")

**Status:** Concept — fills the flagged **"Dawn = Don" payoff mechanic** (must be *used* in-game, not only mental). Solution word/action lean: **DAWN**; exact latch/payoff not locked.  
**Zones:** Main (recording + Tony face) · Storage (handshake photo) · Office (Dawn-labeled photo + Partner Line desk prop)  
**Type:** Cross-zone triangulation → physical reframe action in Office.  
**Related:** Clue set already sketched in [`../../01-Story/narrative-arc.md`](../../01-Story/narrative-arc.md); copy drafts in [`../../01-Story/in-room-text.md`](../../01-Story/in-room-text.md). Suspicion pacing: [`../../03-Flow/suspicion-curve.md`](../../03-Flow/suspicion-curve.md).

---

## Summary

Players spend Main + Storage building a wrong story: Tony answers to **"the Don."** In the Office they finally get a name — **Dawn Adams** — and must *do something physical* that only makes sense once they've made the connection. The a-ha is not reading a spoiler note; it's realizing the ominous nickname was always a person, then replacing the nickname with her real name on Tony's private "partner line."

Looks like: calling the boss.  
Actually is: dialing / seating your uncle's charity partner — the Chief of Police.

---

## Story Justification

Tony kept the night program off paper. He and Dawn used a private desk line and a running joke of a nickname ("the Don") so casual listeners / Frank / paperwork never spelled out the partnership. The desk still has the old **Partner** slot occupied by a worn card that just says **THE DON**. Once you know who that is, you seat her real identity — and the desk gives up what Tony hid for the next person who figured it out (ledger page, second-half tape, trunk part — TBD).

This puzzle is the **suspicion-curve turn made playable**: same slang, new reading, confirmed by an action.

---

## Design Goal

Keep:
- Existing 4-clue triangulation (Tony face → recording → handshake → Dawn label)
- Hard copy law (never say mob / mafia / hit)
- Office as the turn start (Main/Storage still deepen the wrong assumption)
- An in-game **use** of "Don = Dawn," not a purely mental a-ha

Prefer:
- A physical action that *is* the reframe (swap nickname → real name), not only typing letters into a generic lock
- Optional audio payoff (Dawn's thank-you / second half of tape) so the emotional re-read lands the moment you "call"

## Clue Triangulation (inputs — already in story)

| # | Clue | Zone | Alone, it says… | With the rest… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photo of Uncle Tony (face ID) | Main | "This is Tony." | Lets you recognize him in the handshake |
| 2 | Recording: "ready for the Don… no names, no paper trail… handled by Friday" (+ old man / Corolla texture elsewhere) | Main | Ambiguous slang — players invent crime | "the Don" is a person Tony reports readiness to |
| 3 | Handshake photo: Tony + unnamed Chief of Police | Storage | Friendly photo; easy to misread as corruption once you're already suspicious | Same face as the Office-labeled photo |
| 4 | Photo labeled **Dawn Adams** (Chief) | Office | Gives the Chief a first name | **Don = Dawn**; handshake was partnership, not payoff |

No single clue solves it. Office is required. That is intentional.

## Player Experience (Step by Step)

1. **Main — plant the nickname.** Players hear "the Don" on the evidence recording and clock Tony's face on the wall. Unease grows; nothing says crime.
2. **Storage — peak wrong certainty.** Handshake with a cop, no caption explaining why. Theory feels airtight.
3. **Office — first crack.** Labeled Dawn Adams photo. Someone connects Don ↔ Dawn (phonetic + face match to handshake).
4. **Notice the Partner Line.** On Tony's desk: a small private phone / card cradle labeled something like **PARTNER** or **NIGHT LINE**, currently holding a greasy handwritten card: **THE DON**. Nearby sticky (Tony's hand): *"When it's ready — call the Don. Use the name, not the nickname."*
5. **The realization becomes an action.** Players remove THE DON card and seat **DAWN** (see mechanism options below).
6. **Payoff.** Latch releases / phone plays Dawn's reframe audio / drawer opens — whatever we wire. The room does not lecture; the player just *did* the twist.

## Solution Mechanism (options)

### Option A — Name card swap (recommended lean)

| Piece | Role |
|---|---|
| Desk **Partner** card slot | Mechanical latch behind the slot; only the correctly cut / magnet-keyed card releases it |
| Card currently seated | **"THE DON"** — looks ominous; wrong key (or a dummy that holds the slot closed) |
| Correct card | **"DAWN"** or **"DAWN ADAMS"** — found clipped to the labeled Office photo, in her frame, or as a spare Chief business card in the desk blotter |
| Optional handset | Lifting after correct seat auto-plays Dawn's thank-you snippet (second half of tape) |

**Why it works:** The physical verb *is* the story — tear out the nickname, put in the real person. Fair once triangulated; opaque before Office. Stays mostly mechanical (magnets / card shape / pin tumblers in the slot). Electronics optional for audio only.

**Encoding note:** Prefer the seated face to read **DAWN** (4 letters) if we also want a backup letter-lock path; "DAWN ADAMS" is fine on a full business card if the latch keys off a chip/magnet rather than letter count.

### Option B — Letter board / dial strip spelling DAWN

Four sliding letter tiles or a phone keypad next to the handset. Sticky note: use the name. Enter **D-A-W-N** → green lamp / solenoid. Feels more "code lock," less magical — keep as backup if card-swap build is fussy. Can combine with A (card shows the spelling; tiles confirm).

### Option C — Outbound dial on the shop phone

Shop phone later-use path (already reserved in phone prop docs): players dial a number found on Dawn's card, or letter-map **DAWN** on the keypad. Plays her line / unlocks remote latch. Strong if we want the Main phone to matter mid-game; risk = overcrowding the start-game answering machine prop. Prefer a **separate desk handset** in Office unless we deliberately merge.

### Current lean — **A (+ optional audio)**

Card swap on the Partner Line cradle; audio reframe on success. B as assist/backup spelling. C only if we decide the shop phone needs a second act.

## What It Unlocks (payoff candidates — pick later)

Ranked for story fit:

1. **Office drawer / strongbox** containing the **donor ledger** page ("THE FAMILY — Anonymous Contributions Fund… per T., D.A.") — first paper that refuses the crime reading.
2. **Second half of the evidence tape** (Dawn: *"…it's Dawn. Parts came in for the Corolla…"*) if that half is physically gated rather than free on the same cassette.
3. **One part of the trunk's 3-part lock** — "you don't open the dead thing until you know who Tony really worked with."
4. Hybrid: ledger *and* a trunk token in the same drawer.

**Avoid:** Dumping the entire truth monologue here. This beat should crack *one* major assumption (Don → Dawn / partner not boss) and hand a tool for the cascade (ledger, tape, photos note).

## Difficulty & Fail-Safes

- **Estimated solve time:** 3–8 minutes once Office is open *if* the group already noticed the photos; longer if they must re-listen to the Main recording.
- **Hint escalation:**
  1. "Who is in both photographs with Tony?"
  2. "The recording never gave a name — only a nickname. The Office gave a name."
  3. "Partner line: use the name, not the nickname." (point at the sticky / the seated THE DON card)
- **Reset:** Reseat THE DON card; re-hide/reclip Dawn card on the labeled photo; close drawer; rewind/stop Dawn audio.
- **Mid-game assist bypass candidate?** **Yes.** Tony's Locker can hold a duplicate Dawn business card and/or a slip: *"Don → Dawn. Seat her name on the partner line."* Matches the adaptive-difficulty example already called out for spelling the Dawn/Don link more plainly.
